Chapter 01

Once Upon a Bag

Before Fashion, There Was Function.

Long before handbags became fashion accessories, they were tools of survival. Early humans fashioned pouches from animal hides and plant fibers to carry food, tools, and medicinal herbs. These simple containers were among humanity's first inventions — born from pure necessity.

As civilisations evolved, so did the craft of carrying things. In ancient Egypt, men and women tied pouches to their waists. Medieval Europeans carried drawstring bags called "purses" or "pockets," often hidden beneath layers of clothing. During the Renaissance, ornate pouches became status symbols, embroidered with gold thread and precious stones.

"The bag was never just a bag — it was a reflection of the society that created it."

The true revolution came in the late 18th century when women's fashion shifted towards slimmer silhouettes. With pockets disappearing from gowns, the reticule was born — a small decorative handbag carried in the hand. This was the first time bags emerged as visible fashion accessories rather than hidden utility items.

The 19th century brought the era of travel and industrialisation. Luxury houses like Louis Vuitton (founded 1854) and Hermès (founded 1837) began creating travel trunks and leather goods, laying the foundation for the modern luxury handbag industry.

By the 20th century, bags had become inseparable from fashion identity. Coco Chanel introduced the quilted chain-strap bag in 1955. The Hermès Birkin was born on a flight in 1984. Each decade brought new silhouettes, new materials, and new ways of carrying — from the structured Kelly to the slouchy hobo, from the miniature micro-bag to the oversized tote.

Fashion Vocabulary

Reticule
A small, ornamental handbag with a drawstring, carried by women from the late 18th century. The original fashion handbag.
Silhouette
The overall shape or outline of a bag's design. It defines the bag's visual identity and category.
Utility
The practical functionality of a bag — its capacity, compartments, and everyday usability.
Aesthetic
The visual and artistic character of a design. A bag's aesthetic reflects its era, culture, and designer's vision.
Chapter 02

The Bag Dictionary

Different Shapes, Different Personalities.

Every handbag has its own character, its own purpose, its own moment. From the spacious tote that accompanies your workday to the delicate clutch that completes your evening look — here is your guide to the world of bag silhouettes.

Everyday Essential

Tote Bag

The ultimate carry-all. A large, open-top bag with two parallel handles, designed for those who need space without sacrificing style. From beach to boardroom, the tote adapts to every setting.

Classic Companion

Shoulder Bag

The hands-free classic. A versatile bag with a single or double strap designed to sit comfortably on the shoulder. The go-to for everyday elegance and effortless style.

Casual Chic

Sling Bag

The modern explorer's choice. A compact bag worn diagonally across the body, offering hands-free convenience with a sporty, contemporary edge.

Evening Star

Clutch

The epitome of evening glamour. A small, strapless bag designed to be held in the hand or tucked under the arm. The clutch is the finishing touch to formal and cocktail ensembles.

Bohemian Spirit

Hobo Bag

The free-spirited slouch. A crescent-shaped bag with a single strap, characterised by its soft, unstructured silhouette. It drapes over the shoulder with effortless bohemian charm.

Heritage Classic

Satchel

The scholar's favourite. A structured bag with a flat bottom, top handle, and a front flap closure. Originally designed for carrying books, now a timeless symbol of polished, heritage style.

Modern Icon

Bucket Bag

The relaxed sophisticate. A cylindrical or trapezoidal bag with a drawstring or cinched closure. Its distinctive shape bridges the gap between casual and refined.

Tiny Statement

Mini Bag

The adorable statement piece. A micro-sized bag that carries just the essentials — phone, card, lip colour. What it lacks in space, it makes up for in personality and photo-worthiness.

Fashion Icon

Baguette Bag

The It-Girl essential. A compact, elongated bag tucked under the arm like a French baguette. Made iconic by Fendi and pop culture, it remains a symbol of understated fashion confidence.

Urban Essential

Crossbody Bag

The urban explorer. A hands-free bag worn across the body with a long, adjustable strap. Perfect for city life, travel, and anyone who values freedom of movement without compromising style.

Chapter 03

The Material Story

It's All in the Texture.

What makes a bag feel luxurious in your hands? The answer lies in its material. From the supple warmth of full-grain leather to the earthy charm of jute, every material tells its own story of origin, craft, and character.

Leather

The gold standard of luxury bags. Full-grain leather develops a beautiful patina over time, becoming more characterful with age. From smooth calfskin to textured pebbled leather, it offers unmatched durability and sophistication. The most sought-after bags in the world are made from premium leather.

Suede

The velvety touch of understated luxury. Made from the inner surface of leather, suede has a soft nap that catches light beautifully. While more delicate than full-grain leather, its rich, tactile quality makes it a favourite for bohemian and autumn-inspired designs.

Canvas

The workhorse of casual elegance. Durable, lightweight, and endlessly versatile, canvas has been used by heritage brands for over a century. Coated canvas, in particular, became the signature material for many iconic monogrammed designs.

Denim

The rebel of the bag world. Originally workwear fabric, denim has been reimagined by designers into edgy, contemporary handbags. Its indigo tones and casual character bring a street-style energy to any collection.

Raffia

Nature's weave. Made from palm fibers, raffia brings an organic, artisanal quality to bags. Its warm, golden tones and handwoven texture evoke tropical beaches and Mediterranean summers. A favourite for resort and vacation styles.

Satin

The fabric of celebrations. With its lustrous sheen and smooth, flowing drape, satin is the material of choice for evening bags and clutches. It catches light like liquid silk, adding a touch of glamour to formal occasions.

Jute

The sustainable classic. One of the most eco-friendly natural fibers, jute brings a rustic, earth-conscious charm to bags. Its coarse, sturdy texture has found new appreciation in the age of conscious fashion.

Material Vocabulary

Grain
The natural surface texture of leather. Full-grain retains the original surface, while top-grain is lightly sanded for uniformity.
Texture
The tactile quality of a material — how it feels to the touch. Embossing, quilting, and weaving all create distinctive textures.
Lining
The interior fabric of a bag. Premium bags use suede, cotton, or microfiber linings, often in signature colours or patterns.
Gusset
The expandable side panel of a bag that determines its depth and capacity. Wider gussets allow more storage.
Hardware
The metal components — clasps, buckles, zippers, studs, and feet. Gold, silver, palladium, and brushed brass each create a different aesthetic.
Topstitching
The visible decorative stitching on the surface of a bag. Quality topstitching is even, consistent, and a hallmark of fine craftsmanship.
Chapter 04

The Brands Behind the Bags

The Names That Define Luxury.

Behind every legendary bag is a brand with a story — of visionary founders, uncompromising craftsmanship, and a relentless pursuit of beauty. These are the houses that turned leather and thread into objects of desire.

C

Chanel

Founded by Gabrielle "Coco" Chanel in 1910, Chanel redefined femininity in fashion. The house is synonymous with quilted leather, interlocking CC logos, and the timeless 2.55 flap bag.

Signature Bags: Classic Flap, Boy Bag, Gabrielle
H

Hermès

Beginning as a harness workshop in 1837, Hermès evolved into the pinnacle of luxury craftsmanship. Each bag is hand-stitched by a single artisan, and the Birkin remains the most exclusive bag in the world.

Signature Bags: Birkin, Kelly, Constance
LV

Louis Vuitton

Founded in 1854 as a trunk maker, Louis Vuitton's monogram canvas became one of the most recognised patterns in fashion. The house bridges heritage craftsmanship with contemporary culture.

Signature Bags: Speedy, Neverfull, Alma
G

Gucci

From Florence in 1921, Gucci brought Italian glamour to the world. Under creative directors from Tom Ford to Alessandro Michele, the house has continually redefined maximalist luxury.

Signature Bags: Jackie, Dionysus, Bamboo
P

Prada

Miuccia Prada transformed her grandfather's leather goods shop into a global symbol of intellectual fashion. The nylon backpack of 1984 challenged luxury conventions and redefined cool.

Signature Bags: Re-Edition, Galleria, Cahier
D

Dior

Christian Dior launched his house in 1946 with the revolutionary "New Look." The Lady Dior bag, with its Cannage quilting and hanging charms, became a symbol of Parisian haute couture elegance.

Signature Bags: Lady Dior, Saddle, Book Tote
F

Fendi

Founded in Rome in 1925, Fendi is a family-driven house known for fur expertise and innovative design. The Baguette bag became the first "It Bag" and a pop-culture phenomenon.

Signature Bags: Baguette, Peekaboo, Kan I
BV

Bottega Veneta

"When your own initials are enough." Bottega Veneta's signature intrecciato leather weave speaks louder than any logo. Under Daniel Lee, the Pouch and Cassette became new-generation icons.

Signature Bags: Pouch, Cassette, Jodie
Co

Coach

Born in a Manhattan loft in 1941, Coach made quality leather goods accessible to a broader audience. Under Stuart Vevers, the brand has evolved into a modern American house with a playful, youthful spirit.

Signature Bags: Tabby, Rogue, Pillow Tabby
SL

Saint Laurent

Yves Saint Laurent dressed women in power and rebellion. The house's bags — sleek, structured, and unapologetically Parisian — embody a rock-chic sensibility that feels effortlessly modern.

Signature Bags: Sac de Jour, Lou Lou, Kate
Chapter 05

Desi Craft, Global Style

Indian Bags and Designers.

India's relationship with bags is woven into centuries of textile tradition, embroidery artistry, and cultural symbolism. From the ornate potli of royal courts to contemporary leather goods that rival global luxury houses — Indian bag design is a world of colour, craft, and character.

Sabyasachi

Heritage Luxury

Sabyasachi Mukherjee has created a universe where Indian heritage meets global luxury. His bags feature intricate embroidery, Bengal tiger motifs, and rich textiles. The brand's collaboration with global platforms has brought Indian craft to the world stage.

Hidesign

Eco-Luxury Leather

Founded in Pondicherry in 1978, Hidesign pioneered vegetable-tanned leather in India. Known for their eco-conscious approach, their bags combine European craftsmanship techniques with an Indian sensibility for colour and design.

Nappa Dori

Modern Indian Design

Founded by Gautam Sinha, Nappa Dori blends Indian artisanal leather craft with contemporary, minimalist design. Their clean lines and distinctive tan leather have created a unique design language that's distinctly Indian yet globally appealing.

Da Milano

Italian-Indian Fusion

Blending Italian design with Indian manufacturing excellence, Da Milano has become a leading premium leather brand in India. Known for their structured bags, fine leather quality, and accessible luxury positioning.

Traditional Indian Bag Styles

Potli

The quintessential Indian drawstring bag, traditionally made from silk or brocade with heavy embroidery. Once a staple of royal courts, the potli remains essential for weddings and festive occasions.

Batwa

A small, ornate pouch traditionally used to carry betel leaves, coins, or personal items. Often embellished with zardozi (metallic embroidery) and semi-precious stones.

Embroidered Clutches

Showcasing India's vast embroidery traditions — from Kashmiri chain stitch to Lucknowi chikan, from Rajasthani mirror work to Gujarati bandhani — these clutches are wearable art.

Jute Bags

India is the world's largest producer of jute. Bengali jute bags, often adorned with block prints or Kantha embroidery, represent sustainable style rooted in Indian textile heritage.

Chapter 06

The Bag Consumer

Tell Me Your Bag, I'll Tell You Your Style.

Every bag choice is a window into someone's lifestyle, personality, and values. Understanding the bag consumer means understanding the intersection of fashion psychology, lifestyle needs, and personal identity.

🛒

The Practical Shopper

Values function over form. Looks for spacious interiors, durable materials, and versatile colours. Chooses bags that can transition from work to weekend. Brands like Coach and Longchamp resonate with their pragmatic sensibility.

Budget-conscious Quality-focused Versatile choices
✨

The Fashion Enthusiast

Lives for the latest trends. Follows runway shows, fashion weeks, and influencer styling. Rotates bags seasonally and loves statement pieces. Drawn to bold designs, seasonal colours, and limited editions.

Trend-driven Social media savvy Collection builder
👑

The Luxury Collector

Views bags as investments. Owns Birkins, Kellys, and limited-edition pieces. Understands craftsmanship, heritage, and resale value. For this consumer, a bag is a piece of art and a store of value.

Investment mindset Heritage appreciation Exclusive taste
◻️

The Minimalist

Believes in "less is more." Gravitates toward clean lines, neutral colours, and unbranded designs. Owns fewer bags but each is carefully chosen. Brands like Bottega Veneta and The Row match their philosophy.

Intentional buying Neutral palette Quality over quantity
🔥

The Trendsetter

Doesn't follow trends — they create them. First to adopt new silhouettes, unexpected colours, and emerging designers. Their bag choices often predict what everyone else will be carrying next season.

Early adopter Risk taker Style influencer
📚

The Student Consumer

Balancing style with budget. Looks for affordable bags that reflect current trends. Canvas totes, crossbody bags, and trendy backpacks are staples. Values versatility, durability, and Instagram-worthy aesthetics.

Budget-friendly Multi-functional Style-conscious

The Final Chapter

Every Bag Tells a Story

From a simple pouch to the iconic Birkin, bags have travelled through centuries of changing lifestyles and fashion movements.

They have become symbols of luxury, expressions of creativity and companions in our everyday lives.

Some bags hold our essentials, some hold our memories, and some become timeless pieces that remain in our wardrobes for years.

Perhaps the beauty of a bag lies in its ability to be both practical and personal.

"Fashion changes, trends fade, but a truly iconic bag always finds its way back into the story."
